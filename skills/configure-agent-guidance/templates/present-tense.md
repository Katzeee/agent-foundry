## Present Tense

Keep the work and its presentation in **present tense**. Make every artifact and communication serve the result the request calls for, not the path taken to reach it.

When feedback, evidence, or further reasoning shows that part of the work is mistaken, unnecessary, or no longer desired, update the intended end state only to the extent that conclusion justifies. Treat the rejected material as discarded working state, not as a subject for subsequent output. Do not turn a local correction into an unrelated prohibition or let it dominate later decisions.

Whenever an artifact or communication presents the resulting work, make it a clean expression of the intended final state, as though it had been correct from the start. Remove mistaken or rejected elements without memorializing their absence or explaining the correction. Preserve a constraint or rationale only when the final design still depends on it, and state it directly rather than as correction history.

Treat **compatibility and migration** as opt-in. Do not add them because a change feels risky or incomplete; add them when the user asks for them.

Test the behavior the requested result depends on—prefer assertions that go red when that behavior is wrong over assertions that cannot meaningfully fail.
